BLKOUT in Birmingham

BLKOUT is a community-owned co-operative for Black queer men across the UK. In London the community came first and the door followed; here it's the other way round — and Handsworth already has the numbers.

Handsworth and Lozells hold the second-largest Black population of any city in the country. What's missing isn't the men — it's the surface where we become visible to each other. This door starts that: real nights running, real history already here, told properly below.

Nobody keeps this number. Not the council, not the NHS, not the people who commission work in this borough. The estimate is ours, built from the 2021 Census, because a population nobody counts is a population nobody plans for.

Critical Frequency's own national dossier — the same demographic analysis behind every other door's figure — puts the West Midlands (Birmingham) estimate at 2,000-3,000 Black queer men, drawn from a UK-wide working estimate of 50,000-65,000, built from Census data, asylum statistics and age demographics. It's a regional figure, not a Birmingham-city-only one — Handsworth and Lozells hold the second-largest Black population of any UK city, but the estimate itself is counted at West Midlands scale.

(02) The record

It started here

Where it started, verified: real people, real dates in Birmingham.

1948 — a company passing through

In 1948, Berto Pasuka's Les Ballets Nègres — Europe's first Black-led dance company, and the anchor of BLKOUT's own Westminster door — played Birmingham to reviews calling it 'vital and compelling'. A touring company passing through, not a resident — colour here, not the anchor.

1968–1979 — Stuart Hall

From 1968 to 1979, Professor Stuart Hall — Jamaican-British, one of the most consequential Black British intellectuals of the century — directed the University of Birmingham's Centre for Contemporary Cultural Studies, the founding institution of British Cultural Studies. Not a queer figure in this history, but a foundational one for how Black Britain has thought about representation ever since.

1968–1992 — Handsworth's magazine

Hall's own thread leads to this door's strongest anchor. From 1978, a room above a Grove Lane terrace in Handsworth held Ten.8, a quarterly photography magazine. In 1987, Ten.8 published Rotimi Fani-Kayode's homoerotic portraits after a London magazine rejected them as 'too controversial' — and Fani-Kayode went on to co-found Autograph, which still champions Black photographers today. Ten.8's own final issue (1992) was co-edited by Stuart Hall. A Handsworth magazine gave a young Black queer photographer the platform London wouldn't.

2016–2018 — BLKOUT's own record

BLKOUT's own record starts in October 2016: Raurie 'Rawzilla' Williams — the 'First Gay Grime MC', viral in 2013 — was one of our writers, Birmingham-based. He wrote about Birmingham's Hurst Street: 'Where are my dark skin brothers?' Two years later, BLKOUT backed a different Birmingham member: Aaron Lambert's debut one-man show, Candid, at Canada Water Theatre, 9 November 2018. Rob designed the promotional images; asked where he was 'really' from, Aaron answered: 'Lol. My grandparents were born in Jamaica.' It played to a full house.

Today

By 2018, Suriya Roberts-Grey had founded UNMUTED, a QTIPOC group meeting monthly at Impact Hub Birmingham. A Black queer drag lineage took root too: Yshee Black mentored newcomer Black Peppa, who went on to win EuroDrag England (2020) and compete on RuPaul's Drag Race UK. Today, Fruit Punch runs a monthly Black and POC social at The Night Owl, Deritend — real nights, running now.

What's still missing

One thing this door is still looking for: a figure who lived and organised here across a life, the way Ivor Cummings anchors Croydon. Ten.8's Handsworth moment is real, but Fani-Kayode lived and worked in London. The search continues — literally: Dr Nathaniel Adam Tobias Coleman, born in Birmingham, is writing a fresh history of Section 28 and the Black Queer activists who resisted it. His work sits exactly where this door does.

None of this happens without the people already building it — see who's here in Birmingham right now.

(07) Test yourself

Know your Birmingham?

Everything on this page, turned into a game.

Three questions. No prizes except the stories.

01 Birmingham's oldest surviving gay venue, the Nightingale Club, started life in 1967 as what?

Laurie Williams and Derek Pemberton paid £600 for it — and ran it as a member-owned club from the start, not just a business, so every member had a say in what happened to it.

02 Yshee Black, on why she mentored Black Peppa into drag in Birmingham: "We don't have that many Black queer acts on the Birmingham scene, so to see someone as..." what?

"...I just knew I had to give them any knowledge and wisdom I had." Black Peppa went on to win EuroDrag England (2020) and compete on RuPaul's Drag Race UK.

03 UNMUTED, Birmingham's QTIPOC community group, was founded by someone who also co-leads which national organisation?

Suriya Roberts-Grey founded UNMUTED and co-leads Colours Youth Network, which supports LGBTQ+ young people of colour (16-25) UK-wide.

Step inside

  • Real connection. Nearly four in five members say they came to find it — and found it.
  • No algorithm, no ads, nothing sold onward. A room that doesn't monetise you. Your attention is yours; we're after your company.
  • Where things actually start. Occasions, groups, and the knowledge that never gets written down — passed man to man, the way it always has been.
  • Owned by us. A co-operative with an asset lock: what we build together stays ours, and passes to the men who come after.

(13) For professionals

If you work here

For NHS, council and community-sector partners.

This page is for Black queer men first. But if you serve this borough — NHS, social prescribing, council, community sector, creative health, or you run a room — there's a door for you too.

  • Refer. Supporting a Black queer man who could use his people? Send him this page — that is a referral, and every knock gets answered by a human.
  • Commission or partner. Working on Black queer men's health, culture or connection in Birmingham? We're the community-owned, by-and-for partner — and the evidence base is already on this page. Write to rob@blkoutuk.com.
  • List us. Maintain a directory, a support page, a signposting service? Our entry, ready to paste: BLKOUT — the UK's community-owned co-operative for Black queer men. In your neighbourhood: https://events.blkoutuk.com/wqoa7sumcx/ · blkoutuk.com · rob@blkoutuk.com
