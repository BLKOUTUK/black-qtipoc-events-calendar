(01) The record

It started here

Haringey's Black queer history isn't missing. It's foundational, and largely uncredited.

The Black Lesbian and Gay Centre was founded in 1985, and its northern base was an annex of Tottenham Town Hall on Somerset Road. The following year the borough opened the first council lesbian and gay unit in the country, run by Femi Otitoju. And on 2 May 1987 the Positive Images Campaign and Haringey Black Action marched three and a half thousand people from Duckett's Common to Bruce Castle Park under the banner Smash the Backlash — the first demonstration of its kind in Britain to put the lives of Black lesbians and gay men at its centre.

Section 28 became law in 1988, and Haringey was the borough it was written against. That is usually where the story gets told from — the losing end. But the borough was doing the thing first, and people who were there are still here. The rest of it — the rooms, the nights, the photographs in shoeboxes — is still being gathered, and the panel below is where it continues.

The 2021 Census counted around 52,400 Black residents in Haringey. On the estimates we publish, that's 1,200 to 1,700 Black queer men in the borough — roughly the crowd that filled Duckett's Common in 1987, living here right now. These are working estimates and we welcome correction. The point is the scale: we were never a niche. We were just never counted.

(08) Test yourself

Know your Haringey?

Three questions. No prizes except the stories.

01 In 1986 one council opened the first lesbian and gay unit in the country. Which borough?

Haringey — and it was run by Femi Otitoju. The first of its kind in Britain, in this borough, forty years ago. Everything that followed, including the backlash, started from that decision.

02 On 2 May 1987, three and a half thousand people marched from Duckett's Common to Bruce Castle Park. Under what banner?

Smash the Backlash — organised together by the Positive Images Campaign and Haringey Black Action, and the first demonstration of its kind in Britain to put the lives of Black lesbians and gay men at its centre. It went down The Roundway. People still living here were on it.

03 Which Tottenham MP stood up against Section 28?

Bernie Grant, elected for Tottenham in 1987, opposed Clause 28 in Parliament. The arts centre on Tottenham Green carries his name, and it is still one of the borough's great Black cultural rooms.

(09) Read further

Reading the borough

Haringey's Black queer record is unusually well kept — because people here did the keeping. Start with these, and tell us what belongs on the shelf.

  • Haringey Vanguard — Veronica McKenzie, 2019-2021. Oral histories of the borough's Black and minority ethnic LGBTQ+ activists, 1970s to 1990s. The project closed with an exhibition at Bruce Castle in February 2021; its archive is held at Bruce Castle Museum and London Metropolitan Archives. Visit the project
  • LGBTQ+ timeline, 1967 to 1988 — Haringey Council, 2021. The borough's own record of its activism, year by year — the Lesbian and Gay Unit, Positive Images, Smash the Backlash, and the people who carried them. Read the timeline
  • Challenging Heterosexism: The Haringey Experiment, 1986-1987 — NOTCHES, 2014. How a borough's schools policy became the country's argument, and then its law.

(10) Questions

Asked, answered

Where can I meet other Black queer men in Haringey?

Start with the door on this page — you'll hear about everything as it happens. Wise Thoughts runs a regular LGBTQI+ drop-in at Wood Green, and BLKOUT's national community is open to you today. If a few Haringey men want a table in the borough, we'll help hold it.

Where can I get an STI test or PrEP in Haringey?

Sexual Health London posts free test kits and PrEP to any London address, no GP and no conversation required. The borough's own clinics are listed above, and 56 Dean Street is a short run down the Piccadilly line.

Is BLKOUT a Haringey organisation?

BLKOUT is one community-owned co-operative for Black queer men across the UK — with a door in every borough. We're registered in Croydon and we belong wherever our men are. Haringey has two of our directors living in it.

I'm not out. Can I still come?

Yes. Come as you are — nobody at this door asks you to be anything first, and your business stays yours.

Is the history on this page accurate?

It's checked against the borough's own record and the public archive, and dated so you can see when we last looked. If we've got something wrong — or missed something you were part of — the panel at the bottom is where you tell us, and the page changes.

(12) The members' room

Step inside

The door is how we find each other. BLKOUTHUB is where we live.

The members' room: Black queer men across the UK, owned by the men in it. Why knock:

  • Real connection. Nearly four in five members say they came to find it — and found it.
  • No algorithm, no ads, nothing sold onward. A room that doesn't monetise you. Your attention is yours; we're after your company.
  • Where things actually start. Occasions, groups, and the knowledge that never gets written down — passed man to man, the way it always has been.
  • Owned by us. A co-operative with an asset lock: what we build together stays ours, and passes to the men who come after.

(14) For professionals

If you work here

This page is for Black queer men first. But if you serve this borough — NHS, social prescribing, council, community sector, creative health, or you run a room — there's a door for you too.

  • Refer. Supporting a Black queer man who could use his people? Send him this page — that is a referral, and every knock gets answered by a human.
  • Commission or partner. Working on Black queer men's health, culture or connection in Haringey? We're the community-owned, by-and-for partner — and the evidence base is already on this page. Write to rob@blkoutuk.com.
  • List us. Maintain a directory, a support page, a signposting service? Our entry, ready to paste: BLKOUT — the UK's community-owned co-operative for Black queer men. In your neighbourhood: https://events.blkoutuk.com/haringey/ · blkoutuk.com · rob@blkoutuk.com
  • Run a room. A venue, night or group our men should know about? Tell us — the page gets truer, and it points at you.
